SUBMISSION 1: CNCF Sandbox Journey (BEST FIT)
----------------------------------------------

Title:
From Community Project to CNCF Sandbox: What We Learned Bringing Apicurio Registry to the Foundation

Session Type:
Experience Report

Abstract:
Getting accepted into the CNCF Sandbox is a milestone but the months of governance restructuring, community alignment, and technical changes that precede it are rarely discussed publicly. This talk pulls back the curtain on what the journey looks like from a maintainer's perspective.

I'll walk through the concrete steps we took to bring Apicurio Registry into the CNCF: aligning governance with foundation expectations, adapting our contribution model, navigating due diligence, and the technical changes to meet cloud-native standards. I'll also cover what changed after acceptance in contributor engagement, project visibility, and new responsibilities.

This isn't a clean success story. I'll cover the governance debates we had, the features we deprioritized to meet cloud-native standards, and the cultural shift from corporate-backed project to foundation-hosted community. Many of these lessons apply directly to InnerSource programs transitioning to open governance models.

Takeaways: realistic roadmap for foundation submission, lessons on governance and community structure, honest assessment of benefits and new responsibilities, parallels to InnerSource → Open Source transitions.

Audience:
Open source maintainers, InnerSource program leaders, engineering managers


SUBMISSION 2: OSS Community Maintenance
----------------------------------------

Title:
Building the Code, Nurturing the People: Overcoming Challenges in Open Source Community Management

Session Type:
Experience Report

Abstract:
Maintaining open-source software isn't just about merging PRs and fixing bugs. The hardest problems are human: onboarding contributors who disappear after one commit, aligning roadmaps across companies with competing priorities, preventing burnout in core maintainers, and building consensus when stakeholders have fundamentally different needs.

In this talk I'll share lessons from maintaining Apicurio Registry, a CNCF sandbox project with contributors from Red Hat, IBM, and the broader community. I'll cover what worked (mentorship programs, clear contribution paths, transparent governance), what failed (assumption that documentation solves onboarding, over-reliance on synchronous communication), and the organizational patterns that help sustain healthy communities long-term.

This is relevant to InnerSource programs facing similar challenges: how do you build a contribution culture across teams? How do you prevent your internal commons from becoming a support burden for one team? How do you transition from "this is my team's repo" to "this is our company's shared infrastructure"?

Takeaways: practical patterns for community health, lessons on governance that apply to both OSS and InnerSource, honest discussion of what maintainer burnout looks like and how to prevent it.

Audience:
InnerSource program leaders, open source maintainers, engineering managers


Speaker Bio:
Carles Arnal is a Principal Software Engineer at Red Hat working on the API space. He's also an associate professor at BarcelonaTech and an active committer of Quarkus with over 10 years of industry experience. He maintains Apicurio Registry, a CNCF sandbox project, and has navigated the transition from corporate-backed project to foundation-hosted community.
