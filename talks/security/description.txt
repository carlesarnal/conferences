Title: One Keycloak Realm, Separate Trust Boundaries: Securing Kafka and Its Schema Registry

A producer can authenticate to Kafka and still have far too much power in the schema registry. A consumer can hold a valid token and still be denied its topic. Putting one identity provider in front of a data platform simplifies credentials, but it doesn't make authorization consistent on its own.

This session follows a producer and a consumer through a Kafka platform built with Keycloak, Strimzi and Apicurio Registry. We'll separate token issuance and validation, Kafka principal mapping and ACLs, and registry permissions, then trace one authorized request and one denial live. We finish with what usually breaks in production: token expiry in long-running consumers, signing-key rotation, and who is allowed to change a schema.

Attendees will leave with:
- A map of the authentication and authorization boundaries in a Kafka platform
- How to tell a token problem from a permission problem quickly
- A checklist for topic access, schema access and credential lifecycle

CNCF projects: Keycloak, Strimzi, Apicurio Registry.
