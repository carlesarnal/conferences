Title: Beyond the First Pull Request: Making Contribution Work Sustainable

A pull request has been quiet for two weeks. Is the contributor inactive, or are they waiting for a maintainer? A stale bot that cannot tell the difference can ask the wrong person to act. Sustainable contribution starts by making the next action and its owner explicit.

Using Apicurio Registry's published PR lifecycle and a public pull request I reviewed, we examine three hand-offs: finding a reviewer, responding to requested changes and moving an approved change through verification. The documented policy distinguishes waiting on the author from waiting on a maintainer: maintainer-only delays receive an overdue signal rather than automatically closing the contributor's work. Review assignment considers both relevant experience and load.

We turn that workflow into a small state table: owner, evidence required, next action and escalation. The discussion includes ambiguous ownership and the upkeep that automation itself requires. Attendees adapt one hand-off to their own project; no particular bot is required. The public workflow provides concrete design evidence, not a claim that automation has proved contributor growth or eliminated maintainer overload.

Attendees will leave with:
- A checklist for reviewing the contributor path from issue to follow-up
- Ways to make review ownership and response expectations explicit
- A method for evaluating process changes without overclaiming their effect on community growth
