Reviewer comments on the current proposals:
1. Proxy: a clear failure contract grounded in observed outcomes and an authored
   contribution. Show one recovery action without generalizing selective resend.
   The new failure-matrix.txt organizes the evidence; it is not another test run.
2. Lakehouse: useful distinction between health and progress. The new ledger
   makes the takeaway concrete. Populate it during rehearsal and keep the recovery
   observation separate from claims about the underlying cause or latency.
3. Agents: stronger now that the speaker's merged stale-card fix supplements the
   connected demo. Keep the publisher regression as a short evidence segment;
   the typed MCP wrapper is backup, not a third full story. Fix and sample have
   different merge status, documented in EVIDENCE.txt.
4. Security: the separate serializer/registry and producer/broker paths clarify
   the practical contribution. An audience needs observed positive and negative
   controls, not just a proposed permission matrix. Verify them before delivery.
5. Data contracts: the two-unit fixture has an explicit expected result. Show
   how units are declared and which check fails when normalization is omitted;
   otherwise the semantic claim remains a useful but unimplemented exercise.
6. Prompts: identifying the artifact selected for a response makes rollback
   observable. Implement that provenance and refresh behavior, then evaluate
   instruction changes with recorded model/settings and fixed criteria.
7. A2A/MCP: compare caller-visible requirements rather than protocol marketing.
   The same-capability implementations and versioned observations are required
   before this becomes evidence-led rather than a conceptual comparison.
8. Streaming: previous implementation and delivery support speaker alignment.
   The proposed experiment is new. Do not assign causal certainty to a timeline
   alone or call replayed source timestamps current ingress time.
9. Search: the cutover target now acknowledges continuing writes and remaining
   lag. Validate insert/update/delete behavior and the retained-history assumption
   before presenting it as an achieved recovery rather than a design walkthrough.
10. Sandbox: the public deferral, renewed vote and onboarding sequence resolve
    the earlier lack of specificity. Do not attribute acceptance to one change
    or assume the speaker personally handled every onboarding task.
11. Maintenance: a published ownership-aware lifecycle and actual review replace
    generic community advice. Explain the both-sides-blocked case and distinguish
    documented policy from measured improvements in retention or review time.

   stack setup. Investigate timing before attributing delay to one configuration.

   The abstract describes an experiment, not results already obtained.

   consequence concrete. Verify normalization checks and enforcement boundaries.
8. A2A/MCP: compare one capability and pinned protocol versions. Do not equate
   the protocol choice with where intelligence lives or assume symmetry of features.
9. CDC/search: new rebuild/catch-up focus is stronger but needs actual recovery
   evidence. Retention, delete semantics and cutover need explicit assumptions.
10. Sandbox: accurate baseline now, but generic until actual application decisions
    are documented. Best fit is a community or project-leadership audience.
11. OSS maintenance: a useful workflow exercise, not yet a distinctive experience
    report. Replace hypothetical examples with Carles's verified experiences.
